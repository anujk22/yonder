## Best-fit path

1. **Next Gen is the primary category.** The founders report that both are 19 and active second-year college students. Next Gen judges the idea, meaningful progress toward working core functionality, thoughtful RevenueCat use, and product/technical care from the video and public source. It does **not** require an app-store release, paid developer account, free trial, or judge promo code. The founders' student status is an eligibility fact; Yonder's intended users remain the broad public deciding whether a trip is worth making. The [rules' Next Gen criteria](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/rules) make no campus-product requirement.
2. **Design is conditional, not presently established.** The category rewards innovation, in-app aesthetics, interaction and animation. Its normal entry requirements include a *publicly released* US-accessible store build with working RevenueCat purchase/ads, and judge access to premium features via trial or promo code. The animated film's polish cannot substitute for the app's design. Build 7 lacks RevenueCat and was rejected September 29 with a 2.1.0 information request. An updated qualifying build and in-app purchase would need review and public release before the deadline. See [rules](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/rules) and [submission guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-submit-your-app-for-shipaton).
3. **Other awards are lower priority on current evidence.** Grand Prize shortlisting uses actual RevenueCat revenue, and Yonder reports no users or revenue. Peace would need a substantiated community benefit and still the standard store/monetization conditions. #BuildInPublic needs actual public posts and a documented effect on the work. Sponsor awards require their particular live integrations or releases. Do not add SDKs or recast Yonder as a campus app to seek more checkboxes. See [criteria](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/rules) and [submission guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-submit-your-app-for-shipaton).

## Useful Devpost draft work before final submission

In the logged-in submission, prepare the five-step draft: check the account's academic email; fill the team/representative details from the founders' actual Devpost accounts; set the project name and a broad-consumer tagline; add the accurate project story and technology tags; choose Next Gen; and stage the public repo URL, icon, and category explanation. Save the draft and inspect which exact fields Devpost requires. Upload the final screenshot and add the public video only once they exist. Do not use placeholders that state a purchase, store release, traction, or cross-device exchange has happened. Selecting another category or adding its store link should wait for its actual qualifying evidence. The [official submission guide](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/how-to-submit-your-app-for-shipaton) describes these draft steps and says the final screen separately confirms rules and terms.

Do not press the final Submit project button or accept its terms as part of draft preparation. After the founder reviews and submits, verify the visible `Submitted` status and public entry before the deadline.
